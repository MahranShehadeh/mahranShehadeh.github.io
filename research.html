Research

Watching single molecules, one photon at a time.

My work sits at the intersection of optics, nanofabrication, and machine learning. The goal: push single‑molecule fluorescence into regimes — higher concentrations, faster dynamics, noisier data — where it was never meant to work, and then make sense of what we see.

Research Highlights

Plasmon‑Enhanced FRET

Overcoming concentration barriers in single‑molecule fluorescence. I design and fabricate gold nanostructures — nanoholes and bowtie nanoantennas — that act as “amplifiers” for light, enhancing photon yield and reaching microsecond temporal resolution.

Nanofabrication Optics

Photon‑Stream Analysis

Photon streams are noisy. I build custom pipelines combining Hidden Markov Models, LSTM deep learning, and Bayesian inference to robustly identify protein states from low‑SNR data.

Python / PyTorch Bayesian Inference

FDTD & Simulation

Before building, I simulate. Using FDTD and Boundary Element Methods, I model electromagnetic fields to optimise antenna geometries for maximum near‑field enhancement and minimal quenching.

FDTD Maxwell Solvers

How it fits together

01 Design & simulate

Model the electromagnetic field around candidate nanoantenna geometries to predict enhancement, quenching, and field confinement — before committing to fabrication.

02 Fabricate & measure

Build the optimised structures by FIB and electron‑beam lithography, verify by SEM, passivate the surface, then run single‑molecule FRET on custom optical setups.

03 Analyse with AI

Feed low‑SNR photon streams through HMM, deep‑learning, and Bayesian pipelines to extract conformational states and kinetics that classical methods miss.

04 Iterate

Close the loop: what the data reveals feeds back into the next generation of antenna designs and analysis models.
